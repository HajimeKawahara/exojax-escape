\section{A fixed quantity at every phase}
The earlier audit showed that changing phase support changes the displayed
blue/core residual. This follow-up asks which complete velocity bands can
actually be measured, and whether the residual direction survives changes
in pixels, order and error treatment. The saved atmosphere, the original
RV and amplitude, the full-data reference, and the fitted GP family remain
the starting point. This is an exposure-level residual diagnosis, not a
new atmosphere or a recovered intrinsic planetary spectrum.

Before examining the new results, the protocol fixes fine phase edges
at 0.30, 0.325, 0.35, 0.375, 0.40, 0.425 and 0.435, and coarse edges
at 0.30, 0.35, 0.40 and 0.435. Blue/core/red intervals are exactly
$[-40,-16)$, $[-8,8)$ and $[16,40)$ km/s in the adopted planet frame.
Each interval is divided into 4 km/s subbins. Within a subbin, native
samples receive the original log-error weights; subbins, retained exposures
and simultaneous orders are then averaged equally. Complete support requires
valid native pixel centers outside both band edges and at least one valid
sample in every subbin, in both orders. It does not imply an infinitely
resolved measurement of a continuous profile.

The primary comparison uses the same complete-band exposures for blue, core
and red. A second product uses the complete support of each band separately,
so the measurable late core/red information is retained. The original
8 km/s, white-error-weighted display is also reproduced, with partial
coverage explicitly labeled. It is a different statistic. A nominal phase
bin may contain only a shorter observed interval; counts and actual phase
limits accompany every band value. No missing subbin is filled or estimated
from a neighboring band.

\section{Reference mixing and uncertainty}
For night/order group $g$, write the archived native log ratios as $y_g$,
the saved baseline as $m_g$, and the full-data fitted grey/slope GP mean as
$B_g\widehat a_g$. Let $P_g$ remove the weighted time-common spectrum at
each native wavelength, and let $A_g$ form a chosen phase/band average.
The displayed residual is
\[
 d_r=\sum_g (L_g)_r(y_g-m_g-B_g\widehat a_g),\qquad L_g=A_gP_g.
\]
The same $L_g$ acts on data and model. A row may include negative reference
weights outside the displayed phase bin; displayed phase bins are therefore
correlated. A time-independent native multiplicative reference cancels,
but a residual in one physical phase/band can affect several displayed rows.

At fixed covariance and baseline parameters, GP mean estimation is linear.
Writing $\widehat a_g=E_g(y_g-m_g)$ gives the residual map
$R_g=L_g-L_gB_gE_g$, and conditional sampling covariance
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(d)=\sum_g R_g V_g R_g^T.
\]
Here $V_g$ is the current white-plus-time-GP covariance before reference
projection. The calculation retains cross-phase and cross-band terms.
Local refitting of the two global RV/amplitude directions is evaluated
separately. It does not re-estimate the atmosphere or validate the GP family.

Empirical time-block multiplier sensitivities use one multiplier for all
wavelengths and both simultaneous orders in a block, with 1, 2, 4 and 8
exposures and each block offset. Blocks do not bridge large cadence gaps.
Their inputs are residuals after reference/GP fitting; those residuals can
contain real phase dependence and have already been reduced by fitting.
Few effective blocks, covariance-family uncertainty and multiple inspected
phase/band rows prevent a calibrated significance interpretation. Conditional
or block-proxy intervals are explicitly descriptive.

\section{Native local corrections and identification limits}
A separate linear diagnostic adds a constant native log-flux correction in
each phase-bin/velocity-band cell. With design matrix $H$ and the existing
GP-marginalized, common-reference-free precision $W_{\rm eff}$, it solves
\[
 (H^T W_{\rm eff}H)\widehat\beta
 =H^T W_{\rm eff}(y-m).
\]
Unsupported columns are removed rather than assigned an estimated zero.
Rank, conditioning, noiseless recovery and the mapping to displayed residuals
are recorded. A second solve profiles the local derivatives of the global
RV/amplitude. These corrections describe the retained native samples under
a chosen finite basis. Constant corrections across a partly observed band
do not measure its missing part, and a lower in-sample score does not establish
a predictive physical model. Features outside this basis remain in the
residual; stellar or reduction variability can also project onto it.

The full velocity coverage, measured residual direction, and unique physical
cause are separate questions. In particular, the disappearance of a residual
in a shrinking visible portion of the blue band cannot establish that the
entire blue peak matches the model. Missing data remain an identification
limit even if a linear correction fit has a finite covariance on its supported
columns.
