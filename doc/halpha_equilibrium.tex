{\Large H$\alpha$ transport: retained result}\\
PR \#15 --- experiment of 26 November 2026
\end{center}

\textbf{Disposition.}
The tested spherical H$\alpha$ coupling was not adopted: its fully coupled
equilibrium worsened the observed fit. The experimental integration, drivers,
tests and generated outputs have been removed from the active tree. This
short record preserves the result; it does not add a current model or claim
that all spherical-transfer models fail.

\textbf{Experiment.}
Preliminary frozen-state geometry, radial-velocity and photon-recycling
probes motivated testing spherical H$\alpha$ transfer in the population and
thermal equations. The three line branches, their on-the-spot replacements
and receiving heat were coupled consistently. The calculation retained eight
radial cells, static complete redistribution and the original fixed mass-loss
rate $1.05127\times10^{13}$ g s$^{-1}$.

The comparison used the same native observation operator and fixed GP
covariance, with the same two global RV/amplitude parameters profiled for
each atmosphere. Larger $Q$ is worse:

All four night/order contributions worsened. Inner branch source functions
decreased by about 15.5\%, while principal upper-layer sources increased by
44--48\%. Their inner/upper contrast fell to 0.569--0.585 of its original
value. This response does not provide the desired upper-layer suppression.

\textbf{Verification and limits.}
The archived CPU evaluation had full residual maximum $1.46\times10^{-10}$
at unchanged GPU coordinates, with physical and conservation checks passing.
Numerical closure therefore succeeded while the fit-improvement hypothesis
failed. These are historical checks, not a new replay from this minimal tree.
Fixed mass loss, coarse radial structure, static transport, fixed covariance
and unresolved physical adequacy limit the conclusion. The older band
averages in the archived report do not use the complete-band target later
defined in PR \#16 and should not be combined with it.
