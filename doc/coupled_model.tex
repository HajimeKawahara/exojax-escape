\section{What this specification means}
This document first defines the equations and acceptance requirements, before
claiming that their implementations or scientific accuracy have been verified.
Its machine-readable source is
\path{mceg/contracts/escape.coupled_hydrogen.yaml}. The appendix is generated
from its Equation records. Source Bindings and supported Assessments are added
only for code and evidence that actually exist. The earlier two-level and local
six-state documents, contracts and execution records remain historical records.

Three closed problems must remain distinct:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A static radiation/atomic benchmark, with temperature and nuclear density
prescribed and populations in statistical equilibrium.
\item A static thermal benchmark, with density prescribed but temperature
solved from the stated energy balance.
\item A finite spherical reactive-flow branch, with specified inflow density,
temperature, velocity and fractions, coupled to the same spherical radiation
geometry. Its mass flux is fixed by the inflow. It is not a transonic
mass-loss eigenvalue calculation.
\end{enumerate}
The later transonic problem requires an unknown mass flux or base speed and
sonic regularity; it cannot impose every finite-inflow condition unchanged.
Neither the finite benchmark nor an arbitrary inflow reproduces KELT-9b by
construction. An atmospheric claim requires matched inputs and an explicit
observable error budget.

Radiation is initially static with complete line redistribution and the

\section{State and energy conventions}
The bound fractions $f_i=n_i/n_{\rm H}$ and proton fraction $x=n_p/n_{\rm H}$
satisfy $\sum_i f_i+x=1$ and $n_e=n_p$. Gas pressure is
$P=(1+x)n_{\rm H}k_{\rm B}T$, so $\mu=1/(1+x)$ follows the solved state.
This is a single fluid with a common material temperature and velocity;
conductive heat flux, magnetic forces and radiation-pressure acceleration are
outside the present equations.
The internal energy includes translational, excitation and ionization energy,
with ground-state neutral H as zero chemical energy. Accordingly, the radiative
heating is the \emph{full} absorbed minus emitted photon energy, not only
photoelectron excess energy. Collisional excitation and ionization must not be
subtracted again as external cooling.

The sampled transfer coefficients and atomic rates must use identical
frequency quadrature, profiles and population conventions. Spontaneous
$2s\to1s$ two-photon decay is an additional escaping channel, outside the
sampled radiation grid: subtract $n_{2s}A_{2\gamma}\Delta E$ once. Its inverse
absorption is omitted. If a resolved two-photon emissivity is later put into
the transfer grid, this extra subtraction must be removed. A full-atom
Planck--LTE assertion is therefore inappropriate for this initial process set.

Higher bound shells are a documented truncation choice. Comparing maximum
principal quantum numbers 3, 4 and 5 measures sensitivity to those choices;
it is not proof of convergence to an infinite atom. No unstated Case-B or
cascade correction completes the omitted states. Free-free must satisfy its
chosen Maxwellian emission/absorption relation. H-minus is an optional LTE
\emph{trace} continuum closure, off by default, rather than a dynamic chemical
network. Its small abundance and applicability require separate checks; charge
and internal-energy feedback from H-minus are not silently claimed.

\section{Which atomic data and approximations are adopted}
The lower six states retain the preceding local model's tabulated rates and
source review. Electron/proton $2s$--$2p$ mixing is included; mixing among the
$n=3$ orbital sublevels remains omitted. Optional shells $n=4,5$ use statistical populations over their
orbital sublevels. Hydrogenic dipole integrals give their spontaneous rates,
with the upper-state weights shown in the appendix \cite{prozesky}. Added
collisions use the Vriens--Smeets shell approximation as evaluated by the
inspected Cloudy C23 routine, distributed among lower states using their
spontaneous branches. The original Vriens--Smeets full paper was unavailable;
equivalence to every original fit is not asserted \cite{vs80,cloudy}.
\path{hydrogen_atom.py} is explicitly an altered Python adaptation for that
routine, and \path{src/escape/_licenses/Cloudy.txt} retains its notice.

All branches in the new internal transfer have their own frequencies and
upper-plus-lower natural lifetimes. This is separate from the preceding
observer-ray model's common unresolved H-alpha profile. The finite frequency
grid resolves continuum edges and line cores; the unnormalized profile
integrals remain diagnostics even though the sampled profiles are normalized.
There is no pressure/Stark broadening or bulk Doppler transport.

The atomic temperature interval is $5802.26\le T\le14000$ K. Optional H-minus
reuses ExoJAX's John fits and narrows it to $T\le10080$ K \cite{john}.
Its bound-free fit covers $0.125$--$1.6419\,\mu$m and free-free fit
$\lambda\ge0.1823\,\mu$m; zero contribution outside those fits is a model
omission, not evidence for zero physical opacity. The trace check requires
$n_{\rm H^-}/\min(n_e,n_{\rm H})<10^{-3}$. LTE attachment is conditional;
stellar H-minus studies are context for this caution, not validation of
planetary LTE \cite{hminus}.
The Kramers bound-free approximation, Gaunt-one free-free expression and
added-shell ionization fits follow the adopted reduced-atom formulas
\cite{garcia}. The cited ionization fits have about 20\% accuracy over their
reported range; a tighter numerical quadrature tolerance does not improve
that physical accuracy.

An atom-size check at $T=9000$ K, $n_{\rm H}=10^{12}\,\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$ and
a $10000$ K blackbody diluted by $W=0.025$ compares the $n=3$ reservoir from
maximum shells 4 and 5. Its predeclared adequacy criterion is a change below
5\%. The computed change is about 11.46\%; the $n=2$ reservoir changes by
5.74\%. Thus these tests do not establish adequate high-state truncation.
The numerical integration tests remain useful while this accuracy requirement
fails; neither result cancels the other.

\section{Finite boundaries and discretization}
At the inner and outer radiation boundaries prescribe only the incoming
characteristics $J_\nu+\sqrt3H_\nu$ and $J_\nu-\sqrt3H_\nu$, respectively.
Their spectra are inputs; outgoing characteristics are solved. In a transparent
slab with no opposite illumination, an incoming intensity $2WB_\nu$ gives a
mean intensity $WB_\nu$. The factor of two must not disappear in a dilution
parameter convention.

Mean intensities and material source coefficients occupy cells; flux moments
occupy edges. Spherical area factors are $A=r^2$ and shell-volume factors are
$V=(r_R^3-r_L^3)/3$, excluding $4\pi$. The transfer equation balances changes
in $AH_\nu$ against $V(j_\nu-\chi_\nu J_\nu)$. Half-cell optical depths enter
the boundary and first-moment relations. Zero and weakly negative extinction
are retained without clipping. A conservative numerical domain requires the
largest accumulated negative optical depth over frequency to be at most 0.25.
This is an engineering limit, not a physical boundary for masers. The reconstructed
face moments must also obey $J_\nu\ge\sqrt3|H_\nu|$, the positivity condition
for the adopted two-direction representation. A transparent, widely extended
outward-illuminated sphere can fail that angular closure even if the linear
energy balance is exact.

The finite flow's right-endpoint state represents each radiation cell. Its
species and energy source increments use $V/r_R^2$, so reactions and material
energy exchange use exactly the radiation shell volume. The momentum increment
integrates spherical pressure, gravity and linear-tide geometry exactly for
the chosen right-cell coefficient. This is a first-order discretization; a small
algebraic residual does not establish spatial accuracy. Any critical crossing
must be reported rather than silently accepted as a selected transonic wind.
The current population initialization requires nonzero photoionization from
the incoming boundary field. Zero incoming ionizing radiation is outside this
implemented initialization domain, even if internal emission or collisions
could support a mathematical ionized solution.

\section{What verification changed during implementation}
Three implementation choices were reconsidered while checking the equations.
First, a continuum starting at 0.1 eV missed a substantial part of free-free
cooling. The present lower limit is $10^{-4}$ eV, with logarithmic subintervals
and an independent bolometric integral test. Temperature roots from the earlier
coarse grid are not retained as accurate results.

Second, incident and outgoing radiation can be many orders of magnitude larger
than their small net exchange with matter. Subtracting their floating-point
values can lose relative accuracy. The solver therefore records the boundary
budget relative to carried flux, the budget relative to exchanged power, and
material-energy change relative to integrated local heating separately. Its
acceptance bound includes both exchange tolerance and carried-flux roundoff;
a small carried-flux error alone cannot hide a wrong material-energy equation.

Third, larger atoms produced a weak Paschen inversion. Rejecting every negative
extinction excluded those otherwise finite roots. The current signed transfer
domain was introduced only with analytic weak-gain, derivative and
realizability checks; clipping that inversion would have changed the model.
Strong gain and angularly unrealizable solutions still fail explicitly.

These paragraphs record an authored code-review account. No archived execution
receipt exists for those earlier exploratory failures, so they are not presented
as reproducible historical numerical evidence. The retained workflow runs record
the current tests, including explicit negative controls and remaining failures.
